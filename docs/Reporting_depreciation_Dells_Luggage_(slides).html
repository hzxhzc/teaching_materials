Recap: how is depreciation calculated?

Dells Luggage, 1 July 2029

  • Shop fittings: $8,000 plus GST, residual value $500, 5 years, straight-line
  • Photocopier: $11,000 including GST, residual value $2,000, 5 years, reducing balance at 27.52%

1 minute think, 1 minute pair, then answers. Calculators are fine.

Q1  Shop fittings: what is the annual Depreciation expense?

($8,000 - $500) ÷ 5 = $1,500 a year

Q2  Photocopier: what is the Historical cost? Then year 1 Depreciation expense and Carrying value?

Historical cost $10,000. Year 1: $10,000 × 27.52% = $2,752. Carrying value $7,248.

Q3  What does each method apply to find the expense?

Straight-line: Historical cost (same every year). Reducing balance: Carrying value (falls each year).
Reporting depreciation: year ended 30 June 2030
Income StatementOnly the amount consumed this year is reported, under 'Other expenses': $1,500 + $2,752 = $4,252.
Balance SheetHistorical cost, less Accumulated depreciation (a negative asset), gives Carrying value.
Accounting equationAssets down $4,252 and owner's equity down $4,252. No cash is paid.
Successive periodsAccumulated depreciation grows every year, and the asset ends at its residual value.

Exit ticket

(a)  Referring to one qualitative characteristic each, explain why a non-current asset is reported at Historical cost, and why it is also reported at Carrying value in the Balance Sheet.

(b)  Explain the difference between Depreciation expense and Accumulated depreciation, and name the report each appears in.

(a) Historical cost: Verifiability (source document). Carrying value: Relevance (decision-making).
(b) Depreciation expense: amount consumed this year, Income Statement. Accumulated depreciation: total consumed to date, Balance Sheet.
Answers

Activity 1

  • Income Statement shows Accumulated depreciation ($2,000, $2,310) instead of year 2 expense ($1,000, $910)
  • Balance Sheet shows only Carrying value, not Historical cost, Accumulated depreciation and Carrying value
  • Shelving $6,000 / $2,000 / $4,000. Laptop $4,000 / $2,310 / $1,690
  • Net profit understated by $2,400 ($4,310 against $1,910)

Activity 2

  • Expenses: gym $2,520, furniture $900, total $3,420
  • Gym equipment: $12,000 / $6,120 / $5,880
  • Office furniture: $5,000 / $1,800 / $3,200
  • Assets and owner's equity both decrease by $3,420. Liabilities: no effect
